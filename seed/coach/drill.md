# Pitch drill

1. **Your SOM is 2% of a $98M SAM in 3 years. What gets you the first 1,000 customers?** (market)
   - Checking: whether the market slide connects to a real go-to-market
   - Strong: one channel, its cost per customer, and how many customers it has already produced
   - Your numbers: TAM $300.96M, SAM $98M, SOM $1.96M.
2. **Dressly has 33,796 ratings. Why will men switch?** (competition)
   - Checking: whether your edge is big enough to beat habit
   - Strong: the pain, with evidence, and the moment someone switches
   - Your numbers: 68 of 226 recent 1-2 star Dressly reviews mention payments and refunds.
3. **What does your 84.75% take rate leave after store pay?** (unit economics)
   - Checking: whether the business makes money per order
   - Strong: revenue and contribution per order, in dollars
   - Your numbers: Per try-on photo: $0.19 price, you keep $0.16, $0.09 after costs.
4. **The model shows a peak burn of $110,800 a month. What do you cut if the raise takes six months longer?** (risks)
   - Checking: discipline
   - Strong: the cut list in order, and the runway it buys
   - Your numbers: Runway in the model: 20 months.
5. **You are raising $1.5M. Walk me through where it goes and what it gets you.** (the ask)
   - Checking: whether the money buys a milestone
   - Strong: the use of funds and the milestone it reaches before the next round
   - Your numbers: Biggest line: Product & AI ($484,000).
6. **Your market size rests on an assumption (share who buy clothing: 100%). Where does that number come from?** (market)
   - Checking: whether you know which of your numbers are guesses
   - Strong: the source, or an honest 'it is an assumption' and how you will test it
   - Your numbers: Every assumption is listed by market.py show.
7. **Dressly's reviews are about payments and refunds. What stops Dressly from fixing that next quarter?** (competition)
   - Checking: defensibility
   - Strong: why the incumbent's business model makes the fix costly for them
   - Your numbers: 68 of 226 low reviews mention payments and refunds.
8. **Alta Daily users complain about bugs and app quality. How do you make sure that never happens to you?** (competition)
   - Checking: operations, not slogans
   - Strong: the mechanism, what it costs, and what happens when it fails
   - Your numbers: 5 of 28 recent 1-2 star Alta Daily reviews mention bugs and app quality.
9. **Who exactly is the customer, and how many of them are there?** (market)
   - Checking: focus
   - Strong: one customer, one number, one source
10. **Who else is building this right now?** (competition)
   - Checking: whether you know the landscape
   - Strong: names, what they do differently, and why you win anyway
11. **What have you proven so far?** (traction)
   - Checking: evidence over vision
   - Strong: numbers: users, revenue, retention, a waitlist, a pilot, with dates
12. **What is your gross margin, and how does it change at scale?** (unit economics)
   - Checking: margin structure
   - Strong: the margin today and the one cost that falls with scale
